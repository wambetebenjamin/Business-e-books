## SLIDE 2 — INTRODUCTION

- Experiential and humanistic approach to counselling
- Emphasizes awareness, present experience and contact
- Understands individuals within their environment and relationships
- Encourages personal responsibility, choice and authentic living
- Focuses on the client's experience in the here-and-now

(Perls et al., 1951; Joyce & Sills, 2014)

## SLIDE 3 — HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

- Emerged during the 1940s and 1950s
- Developed partly in response to limitations perceived in traditional psychoanalysis
- Influenced by:
  - Gestalt psychology
  - Phenomenology
  - Existential philosophy
  - Field theory
  - Holistic approaches
- Emphasized experience, awareness and contact

(Perls et al., 1951; Woldt & Toman, 2005)

## SLIDE 5 — CORE ASSUMPTIONS

CORE ASSUMPTIONS OF GESTALT THERAPY

- People are best understood as whole persons
- Human experience is influenced by the environment
- Awareness is central to psychological growth
- Experience unfolds in the present
- People have capacity for choice and self-regulation
- Healthy functioning involves meaningful contact
- Psychological difficulties may involve interruptions in awareness or contact

(Perls et al., 1951; Brownell, 2010; Joyce & Sills, 2014)

## SLIDE 6 — AWARENESS

- Central concept in Gestalt therapy
- Noticing:
  - Thoughts
  - Feelings
  - Bodily sensations
  - Behaviour
  - Needs
- Focuses on present experience
- Creates opportunities for choice and change

(Perls et al., 1951; Joyce & Sills, 2014)

Visual: Awareness → Choice → Change

## SLIDE 7 — HERE-AND-NOW

THE HERE-AND-NOW

- Focuses on the client's present experience
- Past experiences are explored through their current impact
- Includes thoughts, emotions, bodily sensations and behaviour
- Encourages direct experience rather than excessive intellectual analysis
- The therapeutic relationship provides an opportunity for present-moment awareness

(Perls et al., 1951; Brownell, 2010)

## SLIDE 8 — FIGURE AND GROUND

FIGURE
What is most prominent in awareness
↓
GROUND
The surrounding context and experiences

- Needs and concerns move between figure and ground
- The figure changes as circumstances change
- Awareness helps identify what is most significant in the present

(Perls et al., 1951; Woldt & Toman, 2005)

## SLIDE 9 — CONTACT AND CONTACT BOUNDARY

- Contact: Interaction between the individual and environment
- Contact boundary: Point at which the individual and environment meet
- Healthy contact allows needs to be addressed while maintaining a sense of self
- Contact involves engaging, responding and withdrawing
- Relationships, culture and circumstances influence contact

(Perls et al., 1951; Joyce & Sills, 2014)

Visual: INDIVIDUAL ↔ CONTACT BOUNDARY ↔ ENVIRONMENT

## SLIDE 10 — UNFINISHED BUSINESS

- Unresolved experiences or emotions
- May include:
  - Grief
  - Anger
  - Guilt
  - Resentment
  - Unmet needs
- Can remain psychologically active in the present
- May interfere with healthy contact
- Therapy promotes awareness and processing

(Perls et al., 1951; Joyce & Sills, 2014)

## SLIDE 21 — PERSONAL RESPONSIBILITY

- Recognizing one's own choices and responses
- Promotes agency and ownership
- Encourages authentic decision-making
- Uses language that reflects personal experience
- Responsibility ≠ blame

(Perls et al., 1951; Corey, 2024)

Example:
❌ "You make me angry."
✓ "I notice that I become angry when…"

The counsellor:

- Observes verbal and non-verbal behaviour
- Uses the therapeutic relationship
- Encourages authentic expression
- Explores interruptions in contact
- Promotes responsibility and choice

## SLIDE 24 — EMPTY-CHAIR TECHNIQUE

- Client imagines another person in an empty chair
- Speaks directly to the imagined person
- Brings unresolved experiences into present awareness
- Facilitates expression of unfinished emotions
- May promote new awareness and perspectives

Example:
"Imagine your father is sitting in that chair. What would you want him to hear from you?"

(Joyce & Sills, 2014)

Gestalt therapy can be applied to:

- Unresolved anger

- Unresolved interpersonal experiences

THERAPEUTIC MOVEMENT
Awareness → Contact → Responsibility → Choice → Growth

## SLIDE 27 — STRENGTHS

STRENGTHS OF GESTALT THERAPY

- Promotes self-awareness
- Focuses on lived experience
- Encourages agency and responsibility
- Integrates mind, emotion and body
- Considers person–environment interaction
- Encourages active client participation
- Supports experiential emotional processing

## SLIDE 28 — LIMITATIONS AND CRITICISMS

- Some techniques may feel intense
- Requires skilled and sensitive application
- May be challenging for clients who prefer highly structured approaches
- Experiential techniques can be misused
- Not every intervention suits every client
- Cultural context must be considered
- Ethical boundaries are essential

(Corey, 2024; Joyce & Sills, 2014)
